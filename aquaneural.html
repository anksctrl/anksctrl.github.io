AquaNeural

Autonomous Intelligence for Aquaculture — a concept-stage IoT and AI management system designed to move Nigerian aquaculture from reactive monitoring toward continuous, data-driven operations.

01 / CONTEXT

A business problem framed as a project.

The current Business Case identifies fingerling mortality as a major financial risk in small-scale Nigerian aquaculture. It frames three systemic problems: nighttime oxygen crashes, blind overfeeding and unreliable manual water testing. The project proposes an autonomous system designed around Nigerian constraints including power instability, limited connectivity, bio-fouling and tight farmer margins.

03 / PROJECT STRUCTURE

Four delivery phases, with a decision gate.

PHASE 0 • Q3 2026

Concept Validation

Charter, pilot partner, site connectivity/power survey, regulatory clearance and baseline data.

PHASE I • Q4 2026

Hardware & Infrastructure

Sensor stack, edge gateway, solar/inverter backup and redundancy validation.

PHASE II • Q1–Q2 2027

Software & Lab Pilot

Mobile dashboard, edge logic, AI/CV model, integration and controlled pilot.

PHASE III • Q3–Q4 2027

Live Farm Pilot

Installation, onboarding, three-month monitoring, tuning and gate evaluation.

Governance decision: Phase III ends with a formal GO / NO-GO evaluation against six defined gate criteria. Phase IV cooperative-scale rollout is separate and requires new approval/funding.
04 / BUSINESS CASE

Investment logic before execution.

The Business Case requests ₦3.5M to move from concept validation through a live farm pilot. It includes direct investment analysis, projected savings/revenue protection, a three-year financial model with sensitivity analysis, TAM/SAM/SOM market sizing and alternatives analysis.

05 / RISK & GOVERNANCE

Risk is designed into the delivery model.

The project risk register identifies seven key risks, including team capability, farmer adoption, hardware cost overrun, pilot underperformance, connectivity failure, competitor entry and funding/cash-flow risk.

  • Hardware volatility: local assembly partnerships and contingency planning.
  • Pilot underperformance: predefined gate KPIs and a tuning buffer.
  • Connectivity: site surveys and minimum connectivity criteria.
  • Funding: parallel funding tracks and a minimum viable funding threshold.

Governance is phase-gated: budget release follows sponsor review, while changes affecting cost, scope or key milestones follow formal approval controls.
