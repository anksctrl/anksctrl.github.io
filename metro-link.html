Metro-Link Smart Ticketing Overhaul

A distressed $10M project examined through scope, schedule, cost, governance and earned-value analysis.

01 / DIAGNOSIS

A project in crisis before completion.

By month nine, the project had consumed 95% of its budget while delivering 60% of scope, and the schedule had slipped by four months.

$9.5MActual cost
60%Scope complete
+4 moSchedule slip
02 / ROOT CAUSES

Five compounding failures.

  • Scope creep: Facial recognition was added without formal change control.
  • Organizational structure: A functional structure left the PM with weak authority over IT resources.
  • Critical path: A six-week IT delay cascaded into a four-month schedule slip.
  • Cost estimating: Per-station permit fees were omitted from the baseline.
  • Budget exhaustion: EVM exposed severe cost and schedule underperformance.
03 / EVM

Making project distress measurable.

0.63CPI

SPI was approximately 0.80, confirming the project was simultaneously over budget and behind schedule.

04 / RECOMMENDATION

Protect the core outcome before adding more scope.

The recommended immediate response was to reduce scope back to the original baseline, protecting core payment functionality rather than compounding cost-control failure.

Key lesson: Change control, a complete WBS, schedule-risk management, continuous CPI/SPI tracking and clear PM authority are control mechanisms.
